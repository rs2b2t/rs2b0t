## Live checks

Both passing checks used an isolated client build against the local server at
`http://localhost:8890`. Neither used a production account.

### Seven-key trip: passed

Command: `bun e2e/jivechests-live.ts --minutes 8`.

The account started with 14 banked keys and teleport supplies. It withdrew seven
keys, walked to the chest, opened all seven, dropped junk, teleported to Falador,
banked the rewards and withdrew the next seven keys. The run reached that state
44 seconds after starting the script.

```text
final: opens=7 trips=2 tookOnTrip=7 atChest=true junkSeen=true keys=7
PASS, 7 chest(s) opened over 2 trip(s), 7 keys a trip, junk seen and dropped, none banked
```

The trip counter includes the initial bank visit. This run did not roll a rune
reward or overflow; those cases have separate coverage.

### Full inventory and return for overflow: passed

A temporary live probe started at `(2914,3451,0)` with no keys and a full inventory:
24 rubies, one adamant square shield, and law, air and water rune stacks. The local
`~item` debug procedure added seven rewards to the full inventory, producing
ground items through the same `inv_add` overflow handler used by chest rewards.
Random events were disabled for this controlled check with `setvar macro_event 1`.

The bot dropped the shield, picked up the dragonstone, banked, returned without
keys, collected the remaining rewards in order, then banked and stopped. The final
inventory contained only teleport runes. Observed pickup messages:

```text
[chests] picked up 1 Uncut dragonstone
[chests] banked the haul and took 0 Crystal keys
[chests] picked up 1 Half of a key
[chests] picked up 1 Runite bar
[chests] picked up 1 Diamond
[chests] picked up 1 Ruby
[chests] picked up 1 Rune platelegs
[chests] picked up 1 Coins
```

This checks ground pickup and bank-return behavior with server-created overflow.
The runtime tests separately cover overflow generated by opening the final key.

## Recovery limit

An earlier live probe staged manually dropped rewards with random events enabled.
It picked up the dragonstone, encountered a strange-plant event, banked and
returned, but the remaining pile was gone. Its pickup-order assertion failed.
The captured output does not establish why the pile disappeared.

Recovery depends on the items remaining on the ground until the return trip.
The bot clears a missing pile and continues or stops according to the remaining
keys; it cannot recover items that have despawned or been taken.
